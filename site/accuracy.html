Weather prediction market accuracy

ForecastEx runs daily prediction markets on the high and low temperature in cities across the US. This page scores the ForecastEx prediction market against eighteen alternative forecast systems, among them the National Weather Service point forecast, the National Blend of Models, the Aviation Forecast, statistical guidance from four model families, and models from the US, Europe, Germany, Canada, the UK, France, and Japan, including ECMWF’s AI forecasting system. Every comparison holds the city, the day, and the lead time fixed, and scores each system against the temperature the station recorded.

Average error of the ForecastEx prediction market and each alternative forecast system against the recorded temperature, plotted against hours remaining before the day ends. Bands show bootstrap uncertainty over the sample drawn.

CITY MAP

Compares ForecastEx prediction market error to each alternative forecast system’s error at each of the 24 cities, over the time window a reader would actually check, morning of, evening before, or newsletter hours, with the difference and its uncertainty interval.

SCORECARD GRID

Summarizes each system’s error, bias, and share of forecasts within one degree at four leads, scored against both the official settlement and the National Weather Service’s climate report, with each system’s percent difference from the National Weather Service shown alongside.

CALIBRATION

Checks whether a Yes price of, say, 70 cents paid out about 70 percent of the time, and scores that reliability with the Brier score, split out by lead and by which side of the book is used.

CONVERGENCE

How early each system locks onto the temperature the day ends on and stays there. One city-day is traced hour by hour beneath it as an example.

RECORD LENGTH

The systems on this page did not all start on the same day. Each bar covers the days that system was actually scored on, after thin order books, short capture windows and gaps in the observation record were excluded. A figure that matches systems to each other can only use the days they share, so it starts where the last of them starts.

CONVENTIONS
  • Span. Every figure names the days it drew on. The ForecastEx prediction market has priced daily highs since the exchange opened its temperature board; its lows were too thinly quoted to score for some months after that; and the alternative forecast systems start at different dates. Each system’s span is printed beside it, and every comparison between two systems is made on the days the pair share.
  • Clock. Lead counts down to station-local midnight, the moment the target day ends. An alternative forecast system’s lead is timed to when its forecast was captured, the ForecastEx prediction market’s lead is timed to the ladder snapshot itself.
  • Truth. Every system is scored against the station’s METAR settle, the day’s highest or lowest hourly reading, rounded to the nearest whole degree. A toggle rescores alternative forecast systems against the National Weather Service’s climate report instead, a different definition of the day’s extreme that runs about a degree warmer on highs.
  • Prediction market value. A contract’s Yes price is the midpoint between the Yes bid and one dollar minus the No bid, p = (Yes bid + (1 - No bid)) / 2 when both sides are quoted. The ladder across strikes is forced monotone by pooling any violations, and the ForecastEx prediction market’s temperature is where it crosses fifty cents, rounded up for highs and down for lows to match whole-degree settlement.
  • Forecast value. An alternative forecast system’s value at any moment is its most recent forecast at or before that moment for the target date. By default that value is held at the running observed extreme, so a forecast high is never shown below a high that already occurred, and a forecast low is never shown above a low that already occurred.
  • Sample. Every system is scored on the city-days its own record covers and the ForecastEx prediction market priced, and every comparison between two systems is made on the days the pair share. A fixed sample is available that restricts the days to those the ForecastEx prediction market priced at every hour from 30 to 0, so a curve’s slope cannot come from the sample changing underneath it. Thin order books, short recording windows and days with gaps in the observation record are excluded and counted, and a bin needs at least 30 city-days before it’s drawn.
  • Uncertainty. Every interval on this page is a 95 percent bootstrap over target dates, 1,000 resamples, fixed seed. When a figure shows the ForecastEx prediction market minus a system, both sides are resampled from the same draws, so the difference reflects paired variation, not independent noise in each side.
