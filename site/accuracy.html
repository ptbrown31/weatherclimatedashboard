Weather prediction market accuracy

ForecastEx runs daily prediction markets on the high and low temperature in cities across the US. This page scores the ForecastEx prediction market against twenty alternative forecast systems, among them the National Weather Service point forecast, the National Blend of Models, the Aviation Forecast, statistical guidance from four model families, and models and ensembles from the US, Europe, Germany, Canada, the UK, France, and Japan, including ECMWF’s AI forecasting system. Each is described at the foot of the page. Every comparison holds the city, the day, and the lead time fixed, and scores each system against the temperature the station recorded.

The working paper Improvement of Daily Temperature Forecasts from a Prediction Market (Brown, 2026, PDF) reports this comparison for target days through 21 September 2026, with its robustness tests. This page rebuilds every day, so its numbers move on from the paper’s as days are added.

DETERMINISTIC SKILL

One value per system, scored against the recorded temperature by hours remaining before the day ends. For the ForecastEx prediction market that value is the median of its price ladder. Under the error curve, how early each system locks onto the temperature the day ends on and stays there.

The whole distribution, for every system that publishes one, the ForecastEx prediction market’s price ladder and four ensembles, scored on the same contracts by hours remaining before the day ends. CITY MAP

Compares the ForecastEx prediction market’s error with one alternative forecast system’s in each of the 24 cities, on the morning of the target day or the evening before, as a percent improvement with its uncertainty interval.

SCORECARD GRID

Summarizes each system’s mean absolute error and mean error on the high and the low at three leads, with a CRPS for every system that publishes a distribution, scored against the METAR settle or the National Weather Service climate report.

FORECAST SYSTEMS

Every system on this page. A deterministic system issues one forecast per run. A probabilistic system publishes a spread of outcomes as well as a centre, which is what lets the probabilistic skill figures score it on the same contracts as the ForecastEx prediction market. An ensemble mean is the average of an ensemble’s members, scored as one value. Grid spacing and time step are the provider’s published values for the run behind each row. Update frequency is measured from this record’s own capture where it holds the run times, and otherwise is the provider’s published schedule. The record is how far back this page scores it. Eight systems, the European, American, German, Canadian, UK, French and Japanese models and the European AI Ensemble Mean, are carried back to February from Open-Meteo’s archive of previous runs before their live capture began.
