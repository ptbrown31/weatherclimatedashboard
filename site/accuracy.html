Weather prediction market accuracy

ForecastEx daily high and low temperature markets scored against eleven public forecast tools, the National Weather Service point forecast, the National Blend of Models, the Aviation Forecast, the European, American, German, Canadian, UK, French and Japanese models and the German Statistical Model, on the same city-days at the same lead, against the temperature the station settled on.

LEAD CURVE

Mean absolute error of the market and of each tool against the settle, by hours before the end of the target day, on a matched sample with bootstrap bands.

MARKET MOVEMENT BETWEEN FORECAST CYCLES

How far and how often the market's central value moves between the tools' forecast cycles, when it settles on the day's answer, how it responds to a tool update, and one city-day traced hour by hour.

CALIBRATION AND BRIER SCORE

Reliability of the Yes price against the share of contracts that paid, with the Brier score and its decomposition by lead, for the midpoint, the two-sided books and the Yes bid alone.

CITY MAP

Market error against tool error at each of the 24 stations in the window a reader would use, the morning of the day, the evening before, or the newsletter's hours, with the paired difference and its interval.

SCORECARD GRID

Every system's error, bias and share within one degree at four leads, in the settle frame and in the climate-report frame, with the percent difference from the National Weather Service row and the newsletter's own window beside it.

HOW TO READ THIS PAGE
  • Clock. Lead is the number of hours before the station-local midnight that ends the target day, a tool is timed by the moment the capture held its value, and the market is timed by the ladder snapshot itself.
  • Truth. Every system is scored against the station's METAR settle, the day's extreme over hourly and special reports rounded half up to a whole degree Fahrenheit, and a toggle rescores the tools against the National Weather Service climate report for the same date, which is a different definition of the day's extreme and runs about a degree warmer on highs.
  • Market value. The Yes price at a strike is the midpoint of the Yes bid and one dollar less the No bid, the ladder is made monotone by pooling adjacent violators, and the market's temperature is where the ladder crosses one half, rounded up for highs and down for lows because settlement is strict.
  • Tool value. A tool's value at any instant is its last record at or before that instant for the target date, and the default view holds it at the running observed extreme, so a forecast high is never colder than a high already recorded and a forecast low is never warmer than a low already recorded.
  • Sample. The matched cohort at a lead holds the city-days where all eleven tools have a value and the market has a crossing, thin books, capture-short dates, backfilled rows and days with a gap in the report record are excluded and counted, and a bin needs 30 city-days to be drawn.
  • Uncertainty. Every interval is a 95 percent percentile bootstrap over target dates with 1,000 draws and a fixed seed, and a market minus tool difference is resampled under the same draws so the two sides of it move together.
