INTERNATIONAL STATIONS AND HONOLULU

These stations settle in Celsius, so their labels carry the unit. They take the same encoding as the map above; a grey dot means the view has no value for that station, because US government feeds carry observations everywhere but forecasts only for the United States and, through the Blend of Models, Canada. Each station is named with its observed reading so far and, where the exchange has a listed board for it, the market’s implied median for the day being shown. With no government forecast to compare against, those two numbers are the whole picture abroad. Click a station for its chart.

FORECAST SCORECARD

Where the forecasts disagreed with each other, and where the day actually landed. On a scored day every station sits on one axis of degrees from what was observed, so each tool's distance from the centre is its own error. On a day still ahead there is nothing to measure against, so the axis is the station's consensus median and a wide row is a station the sources could not agree on. Hover any dot, diamond or row for the numbers behind it; click to pin the box.

THE SHADING

The pale field is the NWS forecast, cool blues to warm reds, interpolated between the listed stations. It is derived here, not an NWS product; the official field is the NDFD grid.

A LABEL

Philadelphia 88° (−5) reads: 88°, five degrees below the reference. Labels move out of each other’s way and never cover another station’s dot, so where two cities sit close together one of them may go unlabelled; its dot is still there and still carries its numbers on hover.
